Lesson 11 · 📚 Going Deeper

RAG — answer from your data

A model only knows what it was trained on — not your company's docs or today's data. RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) fixes that: it looks up the relevant information first, then asks the model to answer using it. An open-book exam for the AI.

01 · The Idea

Give the model the right page to read

Instead of hoping the model memorized your information (it didn't), RAG retrieves the most relevant pieces of your own documents and hands them to the model along with the question. The model then answers from that supplied text — accurate, current, and grounded in your data.

02 · The Problem

The model can't know what it never saw

Ask a plain model about your company's private policy and it can't know it — so it may refuse, or worse, make one up. Training data doesn't include your internal docs or anything newer than its cutoff.

▶ ASK A PLAIN MODEL ABOUT YOUR DATA

03 · The RAG Flow

Retrieve first, then generate

RAG adds a lookup step before the model answers. It embeds the question (Lesson 10), searches your knowledge base for the closest chunks, pastes them into the prompt, and then asks the model. Step through it:

▶ RUN THE RAG PIPELINE

04 · Grounding & Sources

Answers you can trace back

Because the answer is built from retrieved text, it's grounded — and you can show which source it came from. This slashes hallucination (the model isn't guessing, it's summarizing supplied facts) and lets users verify.

▶ UNGROUNDED vs GROUNDED

05 · Indexing Your Documents

The prep step that makes it possible

Before any of this works, you prepare your documents once: split them into small chunks, embed each chunk (Lesson 10), and store the vectors in a vector database. That's the searchable knowledge base RAG retrieves from.

▶ BUILD THE KNOWLEDGE BASE

06 · Keep in Mind

RAG done well

🎯 Retrieval quality is everything

If the wrong chunks are retrieved, the answer will be wrong. Good chunks in → good answer out.

📏 Chunk sensibly

Too big wastes tokens and dilutes; too small loses context. Split by section/paragraph.

🔗 Keep it fresh

Re-index when documents change, so the knowledge base stays current.
